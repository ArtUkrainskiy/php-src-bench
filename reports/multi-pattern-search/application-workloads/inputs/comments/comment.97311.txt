Let's look at it another way: as society begins to recognize that an individual's state of mind can result in either success or failure, accommodation should be developed case by case, tailoring the allowances and support carefully to the particular individual, their circumstances, and their capacity to achieve competence in the field they're studying. If that individual can't possibly benefit from their educational program (unless as a personal growth experience) then accommodation should be denied. 

But we have to be careful not to limit opportunity. There's a history of educational institutions steering students wrong for cultural reasons (remember when women could only be nurses or secretaries?) or by underestimating them academically, thereby cutting them off from higher education, when many formerly mediocre  students blossom when they find their niche in a university specialty program after high school.
